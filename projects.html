Project Title: AI Resume Feedback Tool

One-line description: AI reviewer that upgrades student resumes for internship relevance and clarity.

What this visual shows

The mockup highlights the full feedback loop: resume scoring, a before-to-after bullet rewrite, recruiter-facing value, and the next improvement a student should make.

Problem

What issue are you solving? Students submit resumes that are too generic, weak on impact, and hard for recruiters to scan quickly.

Solution

What you built: A resume feedback app that scores sections, flags weak bullets, and generates targeted rewrites.

My role: designed flow, wrote prompts, built evaluation rubric, and tested output quality.

Tools: React, OpenAI API, prompt templates, deployed web prototype.

How it works

  • Input: Student resume + target internship role.
  • AI: Model scores clarity/relevance and rewrites weak bullets using constraints.
  • Output: Improved bullets, section scores, and a prioritized fix list.

Example Output

Before: “Worked on club projects and helped organize events.”

After: “Coordinated 4 club projects and 2 campus events, improving attendance by 35% through targeted outreach.”

Result: Resume impact score improved from 5.8/10 to 8.1/10 in the prototype rubric.

AI Workflow

Prompts

“You are a university career coach. Review this resume for a sophomore applying to software internships. Score each section from 1-10 for clarity and impact. Suggest 3 improved bullet points with metrics.”

Iteration

“Rewrite this bullet point using action + context + measurable result. Keep it under 28 words and avoid buzzwords.”

Improvements

  • Tested STEM and business resumes to reduce inconsistent feedback style.
  • Added section-level scoring rubric to increase reliability.
  • Added stricter rewrite constraints to reduce vague suggestions.

Reflection

What I learned / what I’d improve: Strong constraints make outputs more useful. Next, I would add ATS keyword matching and side-by-side version compare for faster editing.

Project Title: AI Job Description Translator

One-line description: Translates dense job postings into clear student-ready requirements and action plans.

What this visual shows

The mockup explains how a dense posting becomes something usable: plain-language meaning, a student action plan, and clear signals about what proof to show in a resume or cover letter.

Problem

What issue are you solving? Students misread job descriptions and miss key required skills before applying.

Solution

What you built: A translator that extracts job requirements, simplifies language, and creates a practical preparation checklist.

My role: created extraction logic and student-focused output format.

Tools: React, Gemini API, classification prompts, structured JSON output.

How it works

  • Input: Full job description text + student profile.
  • AI: Extracts required/preferred skills and rewrites into plain language.
  • Output: Skill checklist, gap summary, and 2-week prep plan.

Example Output

Required skills: SQL, dashboarding, stakeholder communication.

Plain-language summary: “You need to analyze data, present findings clearly, and work with non-technical teams.”

Action plan: 6 concrete tasks over 14 days (portfolio project, SQL practice, mock presentation).

AI Workflow

Prompts

“Read this job posting. Extract required skills, preferred skills, and responsibilities. Return in JSON with concise language suitable for first-year students.”

Iteration

“Now generate a 2-week preparation plan for this student profile: business major, beginner in Excel, strong communication, limited internship experience.”

Improvements

  • Compared readability for first-year vs senior student profiles.
  • Separated required vs preferred skills to reduce confusion.
  • Added timeline-based next steps for better execution.

Reflection

What I learned / what I’d improve: Structure matters more than long explanations. Next, I would add job-fit scoring and role comparison between multiple postings.

Project Title: AI Cover Letter Feedback

One-line description: Improves cover letters using role-specific context, clearer evidence, and student-friendly revision guidance.

What this visual shows

The mockup frames the tool like a real review experience: weak lines are flagged, the improved paragraph is grounded in evidence, and the student gets a clear next revision step.

Problem

What issue are you solving? Students write generic cover letters that fail to match the role and do not clearly use evidence from their resume.

Solution

What you built: A cover letter feedback tool that grounds revisions in resume facts and aligns language to job requirements.

My role: built workflow, prompt routing, and quality scoring checks.

Tools: React, OpenAI API, response scoring rubric, deployed prototype.

How it works

  • Input: Job description + student resume + draft cover letter.
  • AI: Scores relevance/clarity and rewrites with evidence-based alignment.
  • Output: Improved cover letter draft, confidence score, and revision notes.

Example Output

Before: “I am excited about this role and believe I am a hard worker.”

After: “I’m excited to apply my project coordination experience from leading a 5-person team to deliver a campus analytics project ahead of schedule, and I would bring that same organized approach to this internship.”

Result: Relevance score improved from 6.0/10 to 8.4/10 in internal evaluation.

AI Workflow

Prompts

“Write a focused cover letter for this internship using the student resume and job description. Keep it under 220 words and reference only evidence from the resume.”

Iteration

“Evaluate this draft cover letter from 1-10 for relevance, clarity, and specificity. Then rewrite with stronger alignment to the job requirements.”

Improvements

  • Tested technical and non-technical roles for output consistency.
  • Added grounding rules to block invented achievements.
  • Added concise mode for strict character-limit forms.

Reflection

What I learned / what I’d improve: Grounding constraints are essential for trust. Next, I would add plagiarism checks and recruiter-style feedback simulation.
